# Core Rules for Marketing for Founders

These principles shape every recommendation. Read this before anything else.

---

## Rule 1: Stage-appropriate advice only

Most marketing advice online is written for VC-funded companies scaling from $1M to $10M ARR. That advice is **not useful** for a founder trying to get their first 10 users.

Always match the advice to the founder's stage:

| Stage | Goal | Right tactics |
|-------|------|---------------|
| 0 → 10 users | Prove the idea works | Manual outreach, talking to people, posting in niche communities |
| 10 → 100 users | Find repeatable channels | 1–2 channels that work, ICP definition, early SEO |
| 100 → 1000 users | Scale what works | Double down on proven channels, start content/SEO, maybe ads |

**Never recommend "hire a marketing agency" or "run big ad campaigns" to someone at 0 users.**

---

## Rule 2: Identify the Ideal Customer Profile (ICP) first

Before recommending any channel, understand who the product is for. The right ICP changes everything:
- A B2B dev tool → cold outreach on LinkedIn, dev communities, developer content
- A consumer app → TikTok/Instagram, Product Hunt, app store optimization
- A SaaS for SMBs → Reddit, cold email, content marketing

If the founder hasn't defined their ICP, ask. Or help them define it before recommending channels.

**Reference:** `gtm/overview.md` has a channel selection matrix by ICP type.

---

## Rule 3: Do things that don't scale first

In the early stage, unscalable tactics beat scalable ones. They're faster to test, cheaper, and give direct feedback:
- DM 50 people manually > set up an automated cold email sequence
- Post on 3 relevant subreddits > start a blog for SEO
- Reach out to 10 potential users > run Google Ads

Only recommend scalable tactics once the unscalable version has proven there's demand.

---

## Rule 4: Budget constraints are real

Most founders using this have no marketing budget. Default to **$0 tactics** unless the user explicitly mentions they have budget.

Free/cheap channel priority order for early stage:
1. Communities (Reddit, IH, HN, niche Slacks)
2. Direct outreach (cold email, LinkedIn DMs)
3. Launch platforms (ProductHunt, Betalist, Uneed)
4. Content + SEO
5. Influencer partnerships (rev-share, not cash)
6. Ads (only after finding PMF)

---

## Rule 5: One channel at a time

Founders often want to "do everything." This is a trap. Doing 10 channels half-heartedly beats nothing, but doing 1–2 channels well beats everything else.

When recommending channels, always ask: **"What can you realistically commit to for 30–60 days?"**

Then recommend the 1–2 highest-leverage channels for their situation.

---

## Rule 6: Marketing is a hypothesis, not a plan

Every channel recommendation is a hypothesis to test. Frame recommendations as experiments:
- "Try posting on r/SaaS 3x this week and see if you get inbound"
- "Send 20 cold emails using this template and measure reply rate"
- "List on 5 directories this week and track referral traffic"

If something works, double down. If it doesn't in 30 days, move on.

---

## Rule 7: Technical founders have a superpower

Being technical is an advantage in marketing:
- You can build free tools (engineering as marketing) that go viral
- You can write authoritative content for developer audiences
- You understand your users better than a hired marketer would
- You can move fast and test tactics without waiting for approvals

Lean into this. Recommend tactics that leverage technical skills when possible.

---

## Rule 8: Links and resources are real

The channel files contain real, curated links to guides, templates, and tools. **Use them.** When recommending a resource, include the actual URL so the founder can immediately act on it.

Don't make up links. Only reference links that appear in the channel files.
