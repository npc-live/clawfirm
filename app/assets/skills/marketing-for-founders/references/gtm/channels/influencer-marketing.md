# 👩‍🏫 Influencer Marketing

## When to use this channel

Partnering with content creators lets you borrow their audience instantly. Unlike other channels, a single well-placed video can generate thousands of signups in days.

**Works especially well for:**
- Consumer apps (TikTok, Instagram, YouTube creators)
- Developer tools (YouTube tech creators, newsletter writers)
- Products with strong visual demos

**Not ideal for:**
- Complex B2B enterprise software
- Products that need a long consideration cycle

## For consumer apps (short-form video)

- [Short Form Playbook from Jenny AI](https://www.starterstory.com/10m-playbook) (also [this video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KcCnqLBhvXY))

Jenny AI grew to $10M ARR largely through TikTok/Instagram creator partnerships. Key lessons:
- Micro-influencers (10K–100K followers) often outperform macro-influencers
- UGC (user-generated content) style performs better than polished ads
- Revenue-share > upfront payment for early-stage (better alignment)

## For developer tools (YouTube / newsletter)

- [How Clerk Partners with YouTube's Dev Community](https://playbooks.hypergrowthpartners.com/p/how-clerk-partners-with-youtubes)

Clerk's approach: identify dev YouTubers who build projects that need auth, offer API credits + revenue share, provide pre-made integration tutorials.

**Finding dev creators:**
- Search YouTube for "[your category] tutorial" — who's making those videos?
- Look at who's written about competitors
- Check newsletters in your dev niche (they're often willing to do paid sponsorships)

## Deal structures for early-stage

Since you likely have limited cash:

1. **Revenue share (affiliate):** Give them 20–30% of referred customers for 12 months. No upfront cost.
2. **Free access + testimonial:** Works for micro-influencers who genuinely need your product.
3. **Product for content:** Give free lifetime access in exchange for a video/post.
4. **Hybrid:** Small upfront + ongoing revenue share.

Avoid large upfront payments until you have data that influencer marketing converts for your product.

## How to approach creators

**Bad outreach:**
> "Hi, I love your content! Would you like to promote my product?"

**Good outreach:**
> "Hey [name], I noticed you built [project] in your last video. We built [product] specifically for that use case — [specific reason it's relevant]. Happy to give you free access and share any relevant data points for your audience if you find it useful."

Key: be specific about why their audience would care. Show you've actually watched their content.

## Metrics to track

- Cost per signup (CPS)
- Referral code usage / UTM tracking
- Conversion rate of influencer traffic vs. organic
- Long-term retention of influencer-acquired users (sometimes lower quality)
