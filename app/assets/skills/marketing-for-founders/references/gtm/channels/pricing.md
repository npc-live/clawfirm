# 🎰 Pricing

## Why pricing is hard

Pricing sits at the intersection of positioning, psychology, and business model. Most early-stage founders underprice — afraid to charge too much, defaulting to "cheaper than the competition."

> "Your product delivers value to target customers; with pricing, you capture that value back to build a sustainable business."

## Free trials & freemium

- [Your guide to reverse trials](https://web.archive.org/web/20250814071651/https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/your-guide-to-reverse-trials) — give everyone all features free for 14 days, then downgrade
- [8 fundamentals about free trials for early stage PLG startups](https://www.mrrunlocked.com/p/free-trials-fundamentals)

**Freemium vs. free trial:**

| | Freemium | Free Trial |
|-|---------|------------|
| **When it works** | High volume, viral growth, self-serve | When you can demonstrate value quickly |
| **Risk** | Low conversion, "forever free" users | Users forget to cancel, don't see value |
| **Best for** | Consumer, PLG SaaS | B2B SaaS with clear ROI |

**Reverse trial** (recommended for early stage):
- Start everyone on full features for 14 days
- At day 14, downgrade to free tier unless they pay
- Users experience full value before making a decision
- Higher conversion than standard free trial

## Annual plans

- [14 tactical ideas for selling annual plans](https://web.archive.org/web/20250725183608/https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/how-to-sell-annual-plans)

**Why push annual:**
- Better cash flow (12 months upfront)
- Lower churn (sunk cost effect)
- Customer commitment signal

**How to incentivize:**
- 2 months free (17% discount)
- Extra features only on annual
- Priority support on annual

## Talking to customers about pricing

- [How to have "the pricing talk" with your customers](https://web.archive.org/web/20250717160702/https://www.growthunhinged.com/p/your-guide-to-finally-talking-to)

**The Van Westendorp questions (to test pricing in user interviews):**
1. At what price would this product be so expensive you wouldn't consider buying it?
2. At what price would this be expensive but you'd still consider it?
3. At what price would you think it's a good deal?
4. At what price would it be so cheap you'd question the quality?

Plot the answers to find the "acceptable price range."

## Pricing models for SaaS

| Model | Best for | Example |
|-------|----------|---------|
| Flat rate | Simple products, early stage | $49/month, unlimited everything |
| Per seat | B2B, team tools | $12/user/month |
| Usage-based | API tools, high variance usage | Per API call, per GB, per transaction |
| Tiered | Multiple ICP segments | Starter / Pro / Enterprise |
| Freemium | PLG, viral products | Free forever + paid features |

**For early stage:** Start simple. Flat rate or per seat. You can add complexity later.

## Common early-stage pricing mistakes

- **Too cheap**: Pricing at $9/month when your ICP would easily pay $49
- **Too many tiers**: 5 pricing plans confuses people
- **Features in the wrong tier**: "Enterprise" shouldn't mean "all features" — it should mean "enterprise buying process"
- **Not testing**: Changing price without A/B testing means you don't know what works
- **Free forever without a path to paid**: Freemium only works if there's a clear reason to upgrade
