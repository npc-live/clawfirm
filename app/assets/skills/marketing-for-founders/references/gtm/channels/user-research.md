# ⚗️ User Research

## Why this channel matters

You can't build what you don't understand. User research is how you learn:
- What to build next
- Why users churn
- What your users actually value (vs. what you think they value)
- How to position and message your product

**Talk to your customers. They'll tell you what to build, what to improve, and what to leave alone.**

The challenge: users sign up, learn on their own, and only reach out if they need support. You have to be proactive about gathering feedback.

**The most important survey question for SaaS (PMF measure by Superhuman):**
> "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product]?"
> A) Very disappointed B) Somewhat disappointed C) Not disappointed

If >40% say "Very disappointed," you have strong PMF. ([Full Superhuman story](https://review.firstround.com/how-superhuman-built-an-engine-to-find-product-market-fit/))

## Jobs To Be Done (JTBD) framework

JTBD reframes user behavior: people don't buy products, they "hire" them to do a job.

- [A short introduction to Jobs To Be Done](https://valchanova.me/customer-development-jobs-to-be-done/)

**The switch interview question:**
> "Walk me through the last time you decided to use [product]. What happened right before? What were you trying to do?"

This reveals the trigger (what caused them to look), the context, and the outcome they wanted.

## Practical cadence for early-stage

**Monthly:** Talk to 2–3 customers (20 min each). Mix of new, happy, and churned.

**Quarterly:** Send a 3-question survey to your active user base.

**Always on:** Monitor support tickets and app reviews for recurring themes.

**Tools:**
- Calendly — book research calls easily
- Grain or Otter.ai — record and transcribe calls
- Notion — organize insights and patterns
- Typeform or Tally — send surveys
